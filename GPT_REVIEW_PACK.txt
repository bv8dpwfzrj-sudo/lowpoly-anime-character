=== REVIEW PACK: LOW-POLY ANIME CHARACTER (made with Claude in Blender) ===
Please review this 3D model work. Everything below is plain text so it can be pasted without attachments.
If you can open web links, the GitHub repo with all the renders is: https://github.com/bv8dpwfzrj-sudo/lowpoly-anime-character
Images are in the images/ folder of that repo; lowpoly_character.obj is the full mesh as text;
scripts/ holds the Python that builds the model.

WHAT IT IS
- Original low-poly (PS1-style) anime schoolgirl: navy bob with bangs, long front locks, ahoge and gold clip;
  cream sailor blouse, teal collar, red ribbon, teal pleated skirt, knee socks, brown loafers.
- 894 triangles total (brief: 512-1024, everything counted, no subdivision/hidden geometry).
- Flat shading, one 512x512 hand-painted atlas with nearest-neighbour sampling, Blender 5.3 / Eevee.
- Built vertex by vertex from Python (bmesh); the head is 11 hand-placed 12-vertex rings.
- Head/chin/jaw were the top priority of the brief: projecting chin, defined jaw, jaw-to-neck transition,
  angular cheeks, readable side profile.

WHAT THE RENDERS SHOW (for a reader who cannot see the images)
- turnaround.jpg: front / side / rear / 3/4 full body, textured, plus head close-ups, a grey clay head and a
  black silhouette head.
- head_gate.jpg: head with hair and clothes hidden - grey clay front, side, 3/4, rear 3/4, underside, and black
  side + 3/4 silhouettes. Used to check that the chin and jaw read from geometry alone.
- turntable.gif: 72-frame seamless 360-degree spin, full body on the left, head close-up on the right.

COORDINATES USED BELOW
- Ring table: head frame in cm, menton (bottom of chin) at z=0, f = forward depth (ear axis ~ f=0),
  x = half width. Each ring lists P0 front-centre ... P6 back-centre; it is mirrored to 12 vertices.
  The head is then scaled by 1.08 and moved up to z=131.5 cm.
- OBJ file: world space, cm, Z up, character faces -Y.

QUESTIONS FOR THE REVIEWER
1. Does the chin/jaw/neck construction read correctly from the side and 3/4, given the numbers below?
2. Is the 894-triangle budget split sensibly (hair 33%, head 28%)?
3. What would you change first to make it look more like a polished PS1-era anime character?

=== MODELING NOTES (MODELING_NOTES.md) ===
# Low-Poly Anime Character - Modeling Notes

**Result:** an original low-poly anime schoolgirl. She has navy bob hair with long front locks, an ahoge and a gold clip, a cream sailor blouse with a teal collar and red ribbon, a teal pleated skirt, knee socks and loafers.

* **Triangles:** **894** in total (budget 512-1024), counted from the saved `.blend`'s triangulated meshes. That covers every mesh object; there are no modifiers.
* **Texture and shading:** one 512 px hand-painted atlas with nearest-neighbour sampling, flat shading, rendered in Eevee.
* **Source of truth:** everything is generated by the scripts in `scripts/`. `build_character.py` is the model, and the `.blend` file can be edited directly.

## 1. Reference study
* **Reference ZIP:** full-body front and rear, a rotating turnaround, a face close-up and expression sheets. The style traits I took from it:
  * visible triangles with mostly flat shading;
  * hand-painted, slightly pixelated textures that carry the eyes, folds and pattern;
  * hair made of separate spiky low-poly clumps, with a few protruding spikes that break the silhouette;
  * a simple faceted face whose expression lives in the texture;
  * clothing silhouettes with flared, angular shapes (bell sleeves, flared trousers);
  * 6-6.5 heads tall.
* **Evanciao video:** I could not watch it from this environment; only its title page loads. Its techniques were therefore inferred from the reference images and general low-poly practice:
  * block the proportions in ortho views;
  * use flat shading;
  * hand-paint one texture atlas;
  * build hair as clumps.

  The character, palette, hairstyle and outfit are original. There are no cat-ear tufts, purple bell-sleeve top or flared trousers from the reference.

## 2. Triangle budget allocation (actual)
| Group | Triangles | Share | Objects |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hair | 294 | 33% | cap 84 + 24 clumps (7 bangs, 4 side, 2 long locks, 7 back, 3 crown spikes, ahoge) |
| Head + neck | 252 | 28% | 11 rings x 12 verts + crown fan |
| Clothing | 172 | 19% | blouse/torso 102, sailor collar 14, skirt 24, shoes 2 x 16 |
| Body | 168 | 19% | arms + hands 2 x 48, legs 2 x 36 |
| Accessories | 8 | 1% | ribbon 4, hair clip 4 |
| **Total** | **894** | | 130 spare triangles left in the budget |

The head and hair get 61% of the budget on purpose. The face and hair carry the "anime" read, while the limbs read fine as 6-sided prisms.

## 3. How the chin was constructed
* **Head rings:** the head is 11 stacked loops of 12 vertices each: crown, forehead, brow, eyes, nose/cheekbone, mouth, sulcus, chin, jaw border, throat, neck. Every vertex is placed by hand from a profile table in `build_character.py` (section `RINGS`). This follows the plane-based recipe in `HEAD_MODELING_GUIDE.md` §5.
* **Tilted loops:** below the mouth the loops are lower at the front and higher at the back. This turns one loop, `R8 jaw border`, into the jawline itself, running from the menton up to the jaw angle under the ear. The face's side plane sits above that loop; the jaw's underside plane sits between it and the throat loop `N1`, and the neck column (`N1` to `N2`) starts behind it.
* **Chin projection:** the chin loop's front vertex sits **0.97 cm ahead of the mouth plane**. The anime target is 0.8-1.8 cm.
* **Labiomental dent:** a dedicated ring, `R7a`, makes a 3 mm dent between the lips and the chin, so the transition reads but isn't a trench (anime budget 1-3 mm).
* **Measured from the saved mesh** (`LOWPOLY_CHARACTER_STATS.txt`, head scaled 1.08):
  * cervicomental angle **106 degrees** (target 100-120);
  * lower third about 5.5 cm (target 5-6);
  * labiomental angle about 157 degrees, which is very soft and typical of anime;
  * lower lip about 10 mm behind the E-line. That is above the anime 2-8 mm target, because the low-poly face has no modeled lips: the mouth is painted.
* **Gate checks:** the chin passes the gate with the hair and clothing hidden (`qc/head_gate_sheet.png`):
  * the black side silhouette shows forehead, nose, mouth, chin, underside and throat;
  * the 3/4 silhouette shows the chin and jaw standing clear of the neck;
  * the underside views show the jaw floor, with the neck behind it.
* **No painted chin:** the chin has no painted shadow. Only the neck below the jaw has a light cel-shade band, and the geometry already shows the underside without it.

## 4. What worked
* **Ring tables:** writing each head ring as a half-profile table (P0 front centre to P6 back centre, then mirrored) made the face a deliberate design, not a deformed sphere. Planes (forehead, eye plane, cheekbone, mouth front, chin front, jaw side, jaw underside) are visible facets in clay renders.
* **Tilted jaw loop:** this solves the classic low-poly problem where horizontal rings can't follow a diagonal jawline. The loop *is* the jawline.
* **Triangular hair clumps:** clumps with a triangular cross-section (two inner edges plus an outer ridge) and 2-3 sections each give angular highlights and shadows for free under flat shading. They cost 9-15 triangles per clump.
* **Hair cap:** a low cap under the clumps, ending at the hairline at the front and tucked onto the skin at the sides and back, so no skull shows between clumps.
* **Atlas:** one atlas with a fixed region layout (face front projection, hair/torso/skirt cylinders, leg/arm strips, 32 px colour swatches). Texture details (eyes, blush, folds, pleat lines, sock tops, cuffs) cost zero triangles.
* **Inspection:** the fixed inspection cameras (front, side, rear, 3/4, rear 3/4, above, below, underside), in clay, textured and silhouette, caught every real problem below.

## 5. Problems encountered (and fixes)
| Problem | Seen in | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Hair cap edge made a "tiara" line across the forehead at brow level | first head clay/textured views | front part of the cap now ends at the hairline; bangs hang from there |
| Bangs too short/thin, hair silhouette a narrow helmet | first full view | bangs longer, wider and thicker; side/back clumps pushed out 2-3.6 cm; bigger crown spikes |
| Some clump faces had flipped normals (dark facets) | clay "above" view | orientation reference changed from the path point (which lies on the clump's inner face) to the cross-section centroid |
| Ahoge tip rendered magenta | textured close-up | UV v hit exactly 1.0 and wrapped to an unused atlas area; v is clamped and the texture extension set to EXTEND |
| Cream patch on the middle of the back | rear view | faces straddling the cylinder seam (u 0/1) had their u **averaged to 0.5 = the front texture**; they are now unwrapped to the high side instead |
| Back of the sailor collar hidden inside the torso | rear view | larger offsets and a mid-back vertex so the flap follows the curved back (+2 triangles) |
| Boxy "shoulder pad" arms; long neck; small head | full views | lower/smaller shoulder cap; head lowered 1 cm and scaled 1.08 (anime head-to-body ratio) |
| Two arm/leg/shoe copies missing from the first build | stats | all parts registered in an explicit list (no garbage-collector lookups) |
| Thumb face rejected (duplicate face) | build log | thumb rebuilt as a 3-triangle tetrahedron |

## 6. Limitations (honest)
* **No rig:** no armature or weights are included. The deliverables didn't require one, and the triangle budget doesn't change with rigging. The ring topology (6-12 vertex loops at elbows, knees, neck) is placed so a simple armature with automatic weights would bend acceptably.
* **Fixed expression:** the expression is painted (one face). Expression swaps would need extra face textures or UV offsets.
* **No lips:** the face has no modeled lips, so the E-line metric is above the anime target (see §3).
* **Reference video not watched:** see §1.
